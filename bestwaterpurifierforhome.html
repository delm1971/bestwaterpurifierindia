How to Choose the Right Water Purifier Technology

The primary factor when selecting a purifier is matching the filtration system to your household water source and initial TDS level:

  • Groundwater / Borewell & Tanker Water (High TDS > 300 ppm): Requires an RO (Reverse Osmosis) system combined with UV and UF to filter heavy metals, hard minerals, nitrates, and microbial contaminants.
  • Municipal / Corporation Water (Low TDS < 200–300 ppm): Soft water with low dissolved minerals only needs a UV (Ultraviolet) + UF (Ultrafiltration) purifier to neutralize bacteria and viruses without unnecessarily wasting water.
  • Mixed / Variable Water Source (Fluctuating TDS): Look for purifiers featuring an Advanced TDS Controller or Mineralizer (RO+UV+UF+TDS Control) that automatically preserves essential natural minerals while keeping taste balanced.

Choosing by Family Size and Storage Capacity

Small Households (1 - 3 Members)

A storage tank capacity of 5 to 7 Litres is generally sufficient for daily drinking and basic cooking needs, preventing water from sitting stagnant in the tank for prolonged periods.

Medium to Large Families (4 - 8 Members)

Opt for purifiers with an 8 to 10 Litre storage tank coupled with high purification flow rates (15 to 20 Litres per hour) to handle heavy daily consumption smoothly during peak household hours.

Key Features to Consider Before Buying

  • Water Recovery Rate: Standard RO systems can discard up to 60-70% of intake water. Look for "Eco Water Saver" or high-recovery models that recover up to 45–60% of input water.
  • Copper & Alkaline Boosters: Modern 2026 models feature post-purification cartridges that infuse beneficial copper ions and elevate pH levels for healthier drinking water.
  • Annual Maintenance Costs (AMC): Check filter replacement cycles (typically 12 to 24 months) and local brand service coverage prior to buying.
